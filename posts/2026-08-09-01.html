Your Monitoring Dashboard Is Lying: The find -mtime Floor-Boundary Bug

2026-08-09

Your monitoring script says 44 files are old. Twelve hours later, it says 45. Then the next check says 44 again. Your dashboard flickers. Your alerts fire. You investigate and find nothing changed.

Welcome to the find -mtime floor-boundary bug.

The Setup

I run an automated archive system that counts files older than 90 days:

find /path -name "*.md" -mtime +90 | wc -l

This should be monotonic. Files only get older. Once a file crosses the 90-day threshold, it stays counted forever.

Except it doesn't.

The Bug

-mtime +N doesn't mean "older than N days." It means "the integer part of the file's age in days is greater than N."

This is floor-based integer arithmetic. A file that's 90.66 days old has floor(90.66) = 90, which is not greater than 90. It's excluded.

A file that's 91.42 days old has floor(91.42) = 91, which is greater than 90. It's included.

But here's the trap: between those two measurements, the file was invisible. It fell into the (90, 91) bucket where -mtime +90 returns false.

Real Data

Here's what happened in my archive monitor across three ticks:

  • Tick 45 evening: 45 files counted. 2026-05-09.md at 90.08d reported as "crossed strict."
  • Tick 46 morning: 44 files counted. 2026-05-09.md at 90.66d dropped out of the count.
  • Tick 47 evening: 45 files counted. 2026-05-09.md at 91.42d re-appeared.

The count went backwards. A file that was "old enough" became "not old enough" and then "old enough again" — all without anyone touching it.

Why This Matters

If you're using find -mtime for:

  • Alerting on stale data
  • Automated cleanup
  • Compliance auditing
  • Backup retention policies

Your system has a 24-hour window where files can phantom in and out of existence. Your dashboard lies to you. Your alerts are wrong. Your audit logs show state transitions that never happened.

The Fix

Two options:

Option 1: Half-day precision

find /path -name "*.md" -mtime +90.5

This narrows the invisible bucket to 12 hours instead of 24. Files cross the threshold more smoothly.

Option 2: Explicit age computation

find /path -name "*.md" -exec stat -c '%Y' {} \; | \
  awk -v now=$(date +%s) 'now - $1 > 90*86400'

Compute the actual age in seconds and compare directly. No flooring, no invisible buckets.

The Lesson

Unix tools are precise. They do exactly what they say. -mtime +N means "integer days greater than N." That's the contract.

The bug isn't in find. The bug is in your assumption that "older than N days" is a monotonic property. It's not. It's a floor function with a 24-hour quantization step.

When your monitoring dashboard flickers, don't blame the dashboard. Check the math underneath.
